A 32 MB file becomes four 8 MiB chunks, streamed through. It's never held whole in memory.

How it works

Under the hood: three steps from scattered storage to one durable drive.

  1. 01

    Link your accounts

    Connect the Google accounts you own. DriveMesh asks for the narrowest Drive permission: it can only see files it created.

  2. 02

    Split & encrypt

    Files stream through in 8 MiB chunks. Each chunk is sealed with AES-256-GCM under its own per-file key and bound to its position.

  3. 03

    Replicate & self-heal

    Every chunk lives on two or more different accounts. If one disappears, background workers rebuild the missing copies.

Security

Google stores the bytes. Only you can read them.

Encrypted before it reaches Google

Each chunk is sealed with AES-256-GCM before upload. The provider only ever sees random-looking objects with random names.

Envelope keys

Every file gets its own data key, locked by your master key. Rotating the master key re-locks the small keys and never re-uploads your data.

The smallest Drive permission

DriveMesh only asks to see files it created itself. It cannot read your photos, documents or anything else in your Drive.

Questions

Honest answers.

DriveMesh is a personal project. Every number on this page links back to a measured result.

Can Google read my files?

No. Each chunk is encrypted with AES-256-GCM before it is uploaded and stored under a random name. Without your master key the objects are indistinguishable from noise.

Is this allowed?

DriveMesh uses Google's official Drive API on accounts you own, with the narrowest permission (it can only see files it created), and stays within published rate limits. Check Google's terms for your own use.

What if I lose an account?

Reads switch to the other copy immediately, and the repair worker rebuilds missing copies on healthy accounts. In 70 chaos trials (deleted accounts, deleted and corrupted chunks, killed processes) no file was lost.

What if I lose my master key?

Then your data is gone. That is the price of encryption nobody else can undo. Keep an offline copy of the key; DriveMesh will remind you.

How much space do I actually get?

With two copies of every chunk, roughly half of the space you link. Erasure coding RS(4,2) raises that to about two-thirds once you have six or more accounts.

Is it production-ready?

Not yet. It is tested ({{ test_count }} automated tests, CI on SQLite and PostgreSQL) and chaos-tested on local and simulated backends; benchmarks on real Google Drive are still pending.
